Who this helps, and what it suggests

Before a rover, habitat or drill goes to the Moon, teams test it somewhere on Earth. Picking that test site is a real decision: it has to match the hardware's biggest risk. This ranking gives a planner a quick, sourced way to shortlist sites, and the sliders show how the shortlist changes with the mission's priorities.

  • If cold and polar conditions matter most: the Dry Valleys lead, because they combine the lowest temperatures, a high latitude and permafrost.
  • If impact terrain and ground ice matter: Haughton Crater is a close second and the only impact-formed site in the set.
  • If only dryness matters: push the dryness slider to the top and the Atacama moves to first, which shows why one answer does not fit every mission.

Method

The target

The Moon's south pole, where NASA's candidate Artemis landing regions lie within six degrees of latitude of the pole and include permanently shadowed regions with water ice.

Five traits

Coldest temperature, yearly rainfall, surface material, closeness to the pole, and ground ice. Each is measured for the Moon and for every Earth site.

One score

Each trait is scored by how close a site is to the Moon, then the five are combined with the weights you choose.

score = sum over traits of weight × max(0, 1 − |site − Moon| / range)

The range is the spread of that trait across all sites and the Moon. Surface material and ground ice are rubric scores from 0 to 1 built from yes/no questions: loose fine material, volcanic or basaltic rock, impact-shaped terrain (surface); permafrost present across the site, local only, or not found (ice).

Limits

  • Five sites and one value per trait per site. This is a screening ranking, not a survey.
  • The temperature column mixes definitions: record lows (Dry Valleys, Death Valley), a typical winter low (Atacama), and island-wide or country-wide lows (Haughton, Iceland). The Moon's 38 K is an estimated subsurface value at one cold region.
  • Scores rank these sites against each other. The farthest site on a trait always scores 0 on it, so a 0 is relative, not absolute.
  • Haughton's rainfall is a stand-in from Resolute, Nunavut, which is not on Devon Island.
  • approx. Death Valley's latitude is an approximate figure, and its ground-ice score reflects that no source was found, not a confirmed absence.
  • Surface and ground-ice scores are judgement calls from one page per site. Most values come from encyclopedia pages, so primary papers would be stronger.
  • Terrain slope was dropped because the lunar slope pages found gave only map error figures, not a usable terrain value.
